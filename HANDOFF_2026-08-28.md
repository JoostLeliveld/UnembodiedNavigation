# Handoff — audit, purge, re-run (2026-08-28)

The fixed-path fusion paper. A long session found several real defects in the analysis
chain, corrected some and left others standing. **Every drive-level conclusion currently in
`logs/studies/` is suspect until re-derived.** This file is the plan of record for cleaning
that up. Read `PLAN.md` and `CLAUDE.md` first; this does not replace them.

---

## Part 0 — ground rules for this work

- **Answer from runs, not reasoning.** Execute, read the artifact, then speak. Re-measure
  before quoting any number, including numbers in this file.
- **Mechanisms, not fitted rules.** Never pick a constant by scanning candidates against the
  score it will be judged by — measure the physical quantity that sets it. A "decision rule
  written first" that still reads the outcome is fitting.
- **No synthetic data.** Real Gazebo, real captures. "An unbiased detector" means refusing
  sightings, not simulating them.
- **Before any campaign:** `pgrep -a "ros2 launch|ign gazebo|run_visibility_campaign"` must be
  empty, and the shell must have
  `source /opt/ros/humble/setup.bash && source install/setup.bash` — without it every run dies
  instantly with `Package 'experiments' not found` and is logged as `infra_invalid`.
- **Campaigns load from `install/`**, so `colcon build --packages-select ...` after any `src/`
  edit. New condition ids must be added to `CONDITION_PLANNER` in
  `scripts/visibility_comparison/run_visibility_campaign.py` or the config is rejected.

### Findings that currently look sound

1. **The hull reading is unbiased when the detector sees the whole robot** (+0.17 cm at a
   99–102% height ratio). The lean is truncated boxes, concentrated beyond 16 m where 19% of
   admitted boxes are cut. A 16 m range limit takes the worst camera from 5.8 cm to 1.0 cm and
   keeps 86% of readings.
2. **R should not change.** One commissioned pixel number through each camera's geometry;
   refitting the pixel noise or adding a metric floor over-pads on the held-out drive. Caveat
   below — camera B now sits exactly on the boundary.
3. **The network beats the best single camera ~3x on error and far more on coverage**
   (2.8 m blind against up to 18.7 m for one camera). The four pooling rules span 0.4 cm with
   run-to-run variance removed by construction — a null.
4. **Readings are correlated, not independent.** Averaging 64 consecutive readings buys about
   a factor of 2 where independence predicts 8.
5. **The coupled heading update works live** — heading error 3.49° → 0.69°, published heading
   σ 180° placeholder → 3.10°, position tail 16.65 → 12.20 cm. One seed.

## Part 3 — OPEN DEFECTS. Audit these first, they may invalidate more

**The big one: `fusion_observations.csv` holds ~4 rows per detection.** The manager decides at
20 Hz against a 5 Hz detector, so each detection is republished on about four consecutive
decisions. Measured: 5,544 rows for 1,387 distinct (camera, capture time) pairs — exactly 4.0.

Scripts already corrected to deduplicate: `per_camera_error.py`, `repeating_error.py`.
`replay.py` and `coupled_heading.py` are safe because they bucket on capture time.

**Still counting duplicates — audit and re-derive every number they produced:**

- `learned_measurement_covariance/measure_delay.py` — worse than duplication: it groups
  "instants" by **manager decision stamp**, giving 2,641 groups where there are only 661 real
  simultaneous instants. **Tables 3 and 4 of `RESULTS_SUMMARY_2026-08-28.md` come from
  here**, including "36% of squared error is common" and the per-camera own-error table.
- `fusion_on_fixed_routes/latency_decomposition.py` — no dedupe.
- `learned_measurement_covariance/estimate_r.py`, `fusion_on_fixed_routes/score.py`,
  `ladder_table.py` already go through `aligned`, whose `dedupe` defaults to True — they are
  covered. (An earlier draft of this file said otherwise; that was wrong.)

**The second big one: the logged belief is one 10 Hz cycle older than the truth in its own
row.** Found by the parallel Codex session, verified here on the K3 drive: sweeping a lag over
the truth series, the median belief error bottoms at **0.95 cm at 0.125 s** against **2.86 cm
as logged**. So every belief-level error in this study is inflated by roughly 1.8 cm, including
the belief-error column of Table 1 and the "belief error 2.6-2.9 cm" story, and part of the
belief-level overconfidence I attributed to the independence assumption and to `Q` is just the
logger's sampling offset (`aligned.py` reports NEES 6.78 -> 3.54 from alignment alone).
`aligned.py` fixes this for its callers; anything not going through it is still wrong.

**Other open defects, roughly in order of how much they could move a conclusion:**

1. **`score.py::_fusion_quality` scores per-camera readings at LOGGING time while scoring the
   fused answer correctly** — inflating the single-camera baseline by ~4 cm. "Fusion beat the
   best available camera" is currently unearned. Fix: score each quantity at the instant it
   describes (per-camera at `obs_stamp`, fused at its own `stamp`).
2. **`used == 1` is not a clean per-camera sample.** Acceptance runs through the manager's
   disagreement gate — through agreeing with the other cameras, which is close to agreeing
   with the truth. Report admitted and unconditional side by side.
3. **`belief_correction.compute_update` uses `next_S = S − ΓΣ⁻¹Γᵀ`**, which is only valid at
   the optimal gain. With `gain_scale ≠ 1` the covariance is inconsistent with the mean.
   Check whether `gain_scale < 1` ever fires; if it does, switch that path to Joseph form.
4. **An undocumented tuned constant in the runtime:** `S_upd[0,0] = max(S_upd[0,0],
   0.5*R[0,0])` in `_commit_metric_correction_outcome`. Half of R, origin unrecorded.
5. **The belief publishes a non-informative 180° heading σ** under `camera_xy_only`. Anything
   downstream reading it is reading a placeholder.
6. **Unexplained:** boxes taller than the hull predicts — 21% of readings, +1.2 cm lean. Not
   obstruction. Never chased.
7. **Unexplained tail:** camera A closer than 6 m, and camera C at all ranges, own the p95.
8. **The 16 m admission limit is proposed, not implemented** anywhere in the runtime.

## Part 4 — the re-run plan

Nothing below is quotable from one seed. **Three seeds minimum per arm**, and the campaign
runner kills on wall clock, so size the timeout to the machine on the day: this machine ran at
~0.25 real-time factor on 2026-08-28, needing `--run-timeout 900 --first-cmd-timeout 420`
where 420 s had been enough at 0.5. Check the achieved rate on the first run and adjust.

**Stage A — offline, no machine time. Do all of it before any drive.**

1. Fix `score.py::_fusion_quality` alignment; fix or retire `measure_delay.py`,
   `latency_decomposition.py`, `estimate_r.py`, `ladder_table.py` to use `aligned.readings`.
2. Re-derive every table in `RESULTS_SUMMARY_2026-08-28.md` from the drives on disk. Expect
   Tables 3 and 4 to change materially.
3. Delete the retracted artifacts in Part 2 and grep the repo for the numbers they seeded
   (`3.2 cm`, `0.032`, `3.8x`, `77%`, `5.29`, `2.43`) so nothing quotes a withdrawn figure.
4. Re-run `replay.py`, `repeating_error.py`, `per_camera_error.py`, `gate_ladder.py` and
   rewrite their RESULTS files from the output, not from memory.

**Stage B — the campaign that earns the paper.** One frozen route, everything identical
except the arm. Seeds 0, 1, 2.

| arm | what it isolates |
|---|---|
| single best camera / distance-angle / precisions add / pooled ÷ N | the pooling rule (expected null — say so up front) |
| raw box / fixed offset / hull | the observation model (expected 16x — the real result) |
| `camera_xy_only` vs `coupled` | the heading update (offline says 5x on heading) |
| hull + 16 m admission limit | bias traded for availability |

That is more arms than seeds can afford in one go. **Priority if time is short: hull vs raw
box vs fixed offset (the headline), then H0 vs H1, then the pooling rules last** — the pooling
comparison is the one the replay can already answer without machine time.

**Stage C — what the drives are scored on.** Decide before running, not after:

- localization error and its p95, scored against truth at the instant each quantity describes
- calibration, reported **separately** for samples with a fresh correction and samples during
  an outage — during an outage every fusion arm scores identically (4.51–4.54 cm on the
  held-out drive), so pooling the two only dilutes the comparison
- corrections per drive and longest blind stretch, in **metres** as well as seconds
- heading error and the published heading σ

## Part 5 — context worth not rediscovering

- **The prize is small.** On this route the robot drives blind for 3.1 m of 30 m, longest
  stretch 2.78 m, and dead-reckoning drift is 1.6–2.3 cm/m — so the whole outage costs 4–6 cm
  while two runs of one configuration differ by 0.1 cm. Drift accumulates per **metre**, not
  per second, so driving faster does not enlarge it. This is why arms tie.
- **The replay is the right instrument for a fixed path.** The route is frozen, so what the
  cameras saw is an input; every arm can run over the same stream with zero run-to-run
  variance. It cannot answer anything where the belief steers the robot — that is what the
  drives are for.
- **Two bugs that cost wrong answers today, both worth not repeating:** propagating a mean
  with odometry's *world-frame* increment while the Jacobian says the increment depends on the
  filter's heading (heading then diverges to 22–83°; read the increment in the body frame and
  re-apply with the filter's own heading); and reusing one `CorrectionOutcome` across two test
  arms, because the commit writes its result back into the outcome.
