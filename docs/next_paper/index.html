The deployment gap

The hard part is not using the map. It is obtaining and maintaining it.

DAY ZERO

No detector survey yet

A new camera or facility has no empirical reliability field, but navigation must still decide where it can trust camera localization.

CHANGE

Layouts do not stay static

Racks, pallets, lighting, and camera pose can invalidate an offline field locally. A stale high-confidence prediction is the dangerous failure.

LEARNING

Driving is data collection

Each detection and miss is evidence, but its location is uncertain. The update must preserve failure evidence without inventing precision.

Mean answers: “what do we expect here?”

Calibration and sensed occlusion form a day-zero prediction. It must be judged on unvisited cells and new layouts.

Strength answers: “how much should we believe it?”

Low strength at shadow boundaries and unknown structure lets real driving correct an uncertain prior quickly; high strength prevents isolated noise from flipping clear evidence.

Deployment firewall

Calibration and sensed structure may build the prior. Ground truth and exact CAD score it only. Never leak the offline detector survey into a day-zero condition.

Outcome discipline

Report safety and calibration before path length. A map can look accurate on successful runs while hiding the conditions in which the robot failed.

07 / 08
Scope decision

Commission first. Fuse cameras later.

Two and four-camera layouts are a powerful scale-out test: they reduce blind coverage, but create source-switch uncertainty. Keep per-camera reliability and inflate covariance at an unsupported handover. Do not make this the headline before camera-B detector evidence and a closed-loop handover campaign exist.
