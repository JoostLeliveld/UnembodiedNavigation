IWAI / Wouter-Kouw-adjacent writing style guide
===============================================

Use this as a style target, not as an imitation of a person. The safest style source is the existing `thesis-report` LaTeX text.

General style:
- Clear, compact, and formal.
- Strong distinction between problem formulation, method, experiment, result, and limitation.
- Prefer "we model", "we approximate", "we evaluate", and "the planner uses" over vague passive phrasing.
- Do not use hype words such as "novel", "breakthrough", "robust", or "optimal" unless a precise claim supports them.
- Put the technical caveat near the claim it qualifies.
- A paragraph should usually make one point: motivation, definition, consequence, or limitation.

Preferred paper rhythm:
1. Start from a concrete robotics failure mode.
2. State why ordinary geometric planning is insufficient.
3. Introduce active inference as the mechanism by which observation quality affects action.
4. Separate the abstract planning formulation from the current YOLO/GP implementation.
5. State exactly what differs between compared methods.
6. Present case-study diagnostics before strong claims.
7. End with limitations that motivate the planned experiments.

Sentence examples in the desired style:
- "The learned observability field is not used as an additional reward. It enters planning through the predictive observation covariance."
- "In this work, observability denotes empirical reliability of the external-camera measurement pathway, not formal nonlinear observability."
- "The GP is heading-marginal: orientation-dependent detector variation is averaged into a 2D spatial target rather than modeled explicitly."
- "The present results should therefore be read as evidence that the pipeline can produce the desired behavior, not yet as a statistically complete comparison."

Avoid:
- "The model understands visibility."
- "The planner knows where it can see."
- "YOLO probability."
- "The active inference planner solves the problem."
- "The GP removes noise."
- "The best path."

Use instead:
- "The model predicts detector-score-derived observability."
- "The planner predicts observation reliability."
- "Raw YOLO detector score."
- "The current configuration reaches the goal region in the representative run."
- "The GP smooths noisy target observations under an RBF prior."
- "The selected route under the implemented objective."

Equation style:
- Introduce variables in prose before showing equations.
- Use equations when they explain a modeling choice, not for decoration.
- After each important equation, write one sentence explaining what changes when the quantity is high or low.

Claim style:
- The central claim should be conditional and testable:
  "A learned state-dependent observation covariance can change EFE route choice compared with a constant-observability baseline."
- The current result should be framed as preliminary:
  "Representative runs show collision-free arrival near the goal while using the learned YOLO-score GP without a direct visibility cost."
- The final experimental paper should require repeated seeds, fixed task setup, and explicit invalid-run accounting.
